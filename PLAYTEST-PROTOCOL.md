# Blind pair trials

Status: prepared, not conducted. Recruit three real pairs who have not seen the puzzles. Automated completion is not evidence of enjoyment.

## Consent and setup

Use consenting adults and anonymous pair IDs A, B, C. Do not collect email addresses, private invite URLs, microphone audio or raw chat in the research sheet. Ask separately before recording a screen. Participants may stop at any time. Keep the observer silent except for safety or an explicit request for help; log every intervention.

Give each pair only the public landing URL and this task: “Invite your partner, investigate together, and choose an ending. Use whichever communication tools you prefer.” Do not explain the share button or puzzle solution first. Start one pair untimed and offer the others their own mode choice. Do not compare completion speeds as though these conditions were identical.

At the end ask “When did you need your partner?”, “What was confusing?”, and “What would you remove?” Ask these before suggesting fixes. Report counts as x/3, not broad population claims. Record non-completion as a result, not missing data.

## Fix-before-expansion gate

If any pair cannot invite/join without intervention, misunderstands that clues must be shared, or mistakes AI for canonical testimony, prioritize that observed friction. If two pairs report timer frustration of 4–5, revise the default/onboarding and retest before adding a game system. Log the observation, smallest change, and a new pair's outcome; do not reinterpret an old trial as validation of a new build.

## Separate judge trial

A lone evaluator uses the explicitly labelled solo tutorial. Check discovery, wrong-answer feedback, hint, completion and exit. Its scripted partner and tutorial-only clues never count as a real pair, multiplayer score, or production-case completion.
